# Commercial Proposal & Pricing Simulator

**B2B Sales & Commercial Decision Support — InduTech Solutions (fictional company, synthetic data)**

A portfolio project built to demonstrate the kind of commercial decision-making a B2B
industrial sales/commercial team performs: evaluating a customer opportunity, pricing a
proposal, managing discount and margin trade-offs, assessing commercial risk, and deciding
whether a deal is ready to quote, needs manager review, or needs escalation.

---

## 1. Project Overview

InduTech Solutions is a fictional B2B industrial company (inspired by, but not based on,
any real company's proprietary data) that sells industrial pumps, gas compression
equipment, turbomachinery components, valves, and related maintenance/installation
services to large enterprise customers in Oil & Gas, Power Generation, Manufacturing,
Petrochemicals, Infrastructure, and Renewable Energy.

This project simulates how InduTech's commercial team answers one question for every
opportunity:

> "At what price should we quote this customer while maintaining acceptable profitability
> and managing commercial risk?"

## 2. Business Problem

Sales teams in B2B industrial companies routinely face a three-way tension:

- **Win the deal** (often requires a competitive price / larger discount)
- **Protect margin** (discounting erodes profitability)
- **Manage risk** (payment terms, delivery commitments, warranty exposure, customer
  reliability all affect how "safe" a deal really is)

Without a structured tool, these trade-offs are often made informally, inconsistently
across reps, and without a clear audit trail for why a price or discount was approved.

## 3. Business Objective

Build a commercial decision-support system that evaluates customer opportunities and
recommends a financially viable proposal price by considering costs, discounts, margins,
customer characteristics, payment terms, delivery risk, warranty/service costs, and
competitive pressure — **without automating the final decision**. The system surfaces a
recommended price range, expected revenue/profit, a risk score, a win probability
estimate, and commercial flags; a sales manager makes the final call.

## 4. Why the Problem Matters

- Inconsistent discounting quietly erodes company-wide margin.
- Risk (payment terms, delivery exposure) is often invisible at the point of quoting.
- A transparent, rules-based tool is easier for a commercial team to trust and adopt than
  a black-box model — this project is deliberately built around explainable logic.

## 5. Solution

Three components work together:

1. **Excel Commercial Model** (`excel/Commercial_Pricing_Model.xlsx`) — a live, formula-driven
   workbook a commercial analyst can use directly, with dropdowns for customer/product
   selection and automatic pricing, scenario, and risk calculations.
2. **Python Engines** (`src/`) — the same logic implemented as clean, testable Python
   functions, plus a Logistic Regression win-probability model trained on historical deals.
3. **Streamlit Dashboard** (`app.py`) — an interactive business-facing app with KPI cards,
   scenario comparison, risk matrix, customer analysis, and a downloadable proposal summary.

## 7. Dataset

All data is **synthetic**, generated by `src/generate_data.py` with logical relationships
built in on purpose (not random noise), e.g. higher discounts raise win probability but
lower margin; strategic accounts have higher historical win rates; longer payment terms
raise commercial risk.

| File | Rows | Key columns |
|---|---|---|
| `customers.csv` | 100 | Tier, Industry, Historical_Win_Rate, Payment_Reliability, Customer_Risk_Level |
| `products.csv` | 30 | Base_Cost, Standard_Price, Minimum_Margin, Maximum_Discount |
| `historical_deals.csv` | 500 | Discount_Percentage, Margin_Percentage, Competitor_Price_Index, Won |

## 8. Pricing Methodology

```
Total Cost       = Base Cost + Installation + Maintenance + Warranty + Logistics + Other (× Quantity)
List Price       = Standard Price × Quantity
Quoted Price     = List Price × (1 − Discount %)
Revenue          = Quoted Price
Gross Profit     = Revenue − Total Cost
Gross Margin %   = Gross Profit / Revenue
Minimum Price    = Total Cost / (1 − Minimum Margin)
Target Price     = Total Cost / (1 − Target Margin)
```

Three pricing levels (Minimum / Target / Premium) are compared against the quoted price
and the competitor's price to produce a **margin position** (e.g. "Competitive", "Target
Margin Achieved") — the tool never claims one price is universally "best"; it shows the
margin-vs-win-probability trade-off instead.

Discount tiers (0–5% / 5–10% / 10–15% / 15–20% / 20%+) map to escalating approval levels,
from "no approval needed" to "escalation required."

## 9. Risk Methodology

A weighted **Commercial Risk Score (0–100)** combines eight dimensions, each scored
0 (Low) / 1 (Medium) / 2 (High):

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---|
| Customer Risk | 15% |
| Payment Risk | 15% |
| Margin Risk | 20% |
| Discount Risk | 15% |
| Delivery Risk | 10% |
| Warranty Risk | 10% |
| Competitive Risk | 10% |
| Contract Risk | 5% |

Score ≤30 = Low · 31–60 = Medium · 61–100 = High. Weights and thresholds are configurable
constants in `src/risk_engine.py` and in the Excel model's "Risk Assessment" sheet.

## 10. Win Probability Methodology

Two approaches, both transparent by design:

1. **Baseline (rule-based):** starts from the customer's historical win rate and applies
   explainable additive adjustments for discount, competitive pricing, relationship
   length, strategic-account status, and payment terms.
2. **Logistic Regression model** trained on `historical_deals.csv` (joined with customer
   attributes). **Logistic Regression was chosen over Random Forest/XGBoost deliberately**:
   coefficients are directly interpretable to a non-technical sales audience, the dataset
   is small (500 deals), and explainability matters more than marginal accuracy for a tool
   a sales manager has to trust and act on.

   Evaluation (on a held-out test split): Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, and ROC-AUC are
   all reported by `train_win_probability_model()` — on this synthetic dataset they land in
   the 0.55–0.65 range, reflecting genuine (not manufactured) noise in the synthetic win/loss
   outcomes rather than an overfit model.

Both outputs are explicitly labeled as **estimates, not guarantees**, in the app.

## 11. Scenario Analysis

The Scenario Analysis tab/sheet compares four discount levels (5/10/15/20%) and computes,
for each:

```
Expected Revenue = Revenue × Win Probability
Expected Profit  = Gross Profit × Win Probability
```

This is a simplified expected-value approach — it does not model portfolio effects,
correlation between scenarios, or risk-adjusted discounting.

## 12. Technology Stack

| Layer | Tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial model | **Excel** (openpyxl-generated, live formulas) | What a commercial team actually opens and edits day to day |
| Pricing/risk/scenario logic | **Python** (pandas, numpy) | Testable, reusable, portable logic shared by Excel-equivalent formulas and the dashboard |
| Win probability | **scikit-learn** (Logistic Regression) | Interpretable coefficients for a business audience |
| Dashboard | **Streamlit** | Fast to build, genuinely interactive, easy to demo |
| Charts | **Plotly** | Interactive visualizations inside Streamlit |

## 13. Dashboard

`app.py` is a six-tab Streamlit app: **Executive Summary, Pricing Analysis, Scenario
Analysis, Risk Assessment, Customer Analysis, Proposal Summary** — with KPI cards for
Quoted Price, Revenue, Gross Profit, Margin, Win Probability, Commercial Risk, and Expected
Profit, plus a GREEN/AMBER/RED commercial status and a plain-language recommendation.

## 14. Business Insights (from `notebooks/exploratory_analysis.ipynb`)

- Discount above ~15% is associated with a meaningfully lower average margin.
- Strategic accounts have higher historical win rates than non-strategic accounts.
- Longer payment terms are associated with higher delivery delay (a proxy for strain on
  lower-priority, higher-risk deals).
- Gross profit is concentrated in a few product categories (Turbomachinery, Gas
  Compression) — pricing discipline matters most there.

## 15. Limitations

1. Dataset is fully synthetic.
2. Win probability is an estimate, not a guaranteed outcome.
3. Risk scoring uses configurable business rules, not an actuarial model.
4. Pricing recommendations support, but do not replace, commercial judgment.
5. Expected-profit calculation uses a simplified expected-value approach.
6. Contract/legal terms are not reviewed by this tool.
7. Competitor pricing is a user-entered assumption, not live market data.
